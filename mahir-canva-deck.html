The governance half

The half that is still being written

Malaysia already has its governance principles. They live in the National Guidelines on AI Governance and Ethics, issued by MOSTI in September 2024. Seven strong principles, adopted voluntarily.
  • Fairness
  • Reliability, safety and control
  • Privacy and security
  • Inclusiveness
  • Transparency
  • Accountability
  • Pursuit of human benefit and happiness
The government is now converting them into law through the AI Governance Bill, in public consultation with completion targeted end 2026. MAHIR is our proposal for what that law should require and measure.

The most useful precedent

A delivery rider can already contest an algorithm

Since 31 March 2026 the Gig Workers Act gives workers the right not to be deactivated by an algorithm without recourse, and a tribunal to hear the complaint. KESUMA states it gives greater protection to 3.45 million gig workers.
Parliament accepted the principle. We ask for it to be extended to every Malaysian facing a high-stakes automated decision: in health, in credit, in welfare, in school placement.
Why does a loan applicant deserve less protection than a food delivery rider?

How it works

Five rules are one governance cycle

We manage the risk, we hold someone accountable, we keep a human in command, we protect the system’s integrity, and we always come back to dignity.
We are not asking the country to start over. Enablers E11 to E14 are already written. We are asking for one thing: adopt MAHIR as the plain-language version of that pillar, so every Malaysian can understand it and hold it to account.

M · the ladder

Check the risk before you switch it on

The plan already names this step: enabler E13, National AI Classification. We are saying it should apply before deployment, not after.
TierExamplesWhat is required
UnacceptableViolates fundamental rights or creates disproportionate harmNot permitted
High riskRecruitment, healthcare diagnosis, credit, welfare, law enforcementFull assessment and human oversight
Medium riskEducation assistance, workplace analyticsDocumented testing and review
Low riskRecommendation feeds, basic productivity AITransparency only
M · the assessment

Seven things, checked before deployment

Before a high-risk system is deployed, the organisation assesses seven things. AIGE already calls for evaluation across the AI lifecycle, so this operationalises existing guidance rather than duplicating it.
  • Bias
  • Privacy
  • Cybersecurity
  • Accuracy
  • Explainability
  • The populations affected
  • Potential social harm
M · the human gate

AI recommends. Humans decide.

Not every system needs a person approving every output. Decisions that affect rights or livelihoods do.
The assurance body already exists. The Malaysia AI Safety Institute, MY-AISafe, was launched inside AI Malaysia Berhad on 28 July 2026 with a mandate for safety evaluation and red-teaming.
MAHIR gives it a gate to stand at: we propose its safety certificate becomes a condition of deployment for high-risk public sector AI, not a recommendation agencies can ignore.

The final call belongs to a person

Any decision affecting a citizen’s rights, safety or welfare passes through a human checkpoint before it is finalised. Someone with the authority to override, to suspend, to reject.
Not a machine that decides your fate in silence, but a person who can still stop and think it through. H sits at the centre of MAHIR, and that is not an accident.

R · rights

Respect the person

Anyone affected by an AI decision keeps three things. Malaysia has already accepted this principle in law through the Gig Workers Act 2025.
  • Privacy
  • Human agency
  • The ability to challenge a consequential decision
R · inclusion

Include every Malaysian

The benefit cannot stop at the Klang Valley, large corporations and the digitally confident. This follows the plan’s own direction: AI Nation 2030 is explicit that the benefits must be inclusive, not adoption for its own sake.
  • Rural communities
  • Elderly citizens
  • Persons with disabilities
  • Low-income households
  • SMEs and people with limited digital literacy
R · sequence

Reskill before retrenchment, not after

The usual sequence
  • AI is introduced
  • Employees are displaced
  • Retraining is offered afterwards, if at all
  • By then the income is gone and the household has absorbed the shock
The MAHIR sequence
  • Announce the implementation
  • Identify the vulnerable tasks
  • Run a skills-gap assessment
  • Reskill pre-emptively and redesign the job
  • Deploy human and AI together
Fund it by tying the reskilling budget to a published displacement estimate per sector, routed through the HRD Corp levy employers already pay. Automate the task. Keep the person.

FAQ · part 1

The plan, the framework, and why now

Q1 · What is AI Nation 2030, and how does MAHIR relate to it?
AI Nation 2030 is Malaysia’s national AI strategy, launched 28 July 2026 with AI Malaysia Berhad and MY-AISafe. It sets 5 policy shifts, 14 Impact Engines and 14 Enablers, with E11 to E14 forming the Trust and Governance pillar. MAHIR gives that pillar the same owners, dates and measurable targets the growth side already has, as five plain-language rules.
Q2 · What does each letter in MAHIR stand for?
MAHIR means skilled in Malay. M is Manage AI Responsibly, A is Accountability, H is Human in Command, I is Integrity, R is Respecting Human Dignity.
Q3 · Malaysia has four years until 2030. Why act on governance now?
The runway is exactly why governance cannot wait. Assurance is not delay, it is the condition for adoption, because nobody adopts a system they cannot appeal. The plan itself names Public Trust and Confidence as a foundational enabler, E8. Malaysia is racing to the AI top ten while already among the world’s most-targeted populations for AI-enabled fraud.
FAQ · part 2

What MAHIR adds, and what it costs

Q4 · MY-AISafe already exists. What does MAHIR add?
MY-AISafe does safety evaluation and red-teaming, but today it is advisory. MAHIR proposes one change: a MY-AISafe safety certificate becomes a condition of deployment for high-risk public sector AI, not a recommendation agencies can ignore. A formal gate for a body that already exists.
Q5 · Is MAHIR just more rules on top of laws that already exist?
Every MAHIR rule builds on something that already exists: Enablers E11 to E14, the AIGE guidelines, Cyber Security Act 2024, Online Safety Act 2025, Gig Workers Act 2025, and MY-AISafe. MAHIR does not create a new law or body. It assigns each existing commitment an owner, a date and a metric.
Q6 · Can Malaysia afford to implement MAHIR?
MAHIR uses budget and machinery that already exist. The quarterly trust scorecard is an added section in a report AI Malaysia Berhad must already produce. The reskilling fund redirects money already collected via the HRD Corp levy. The assurance gate uses MY-AISafe, already launched and funded.
Q7 · Voluntary AIGE guidelines are working. Why make MAHIR mandatory?
If an organisation already follows good practice voluntarily, a binding version costs it nothing extra. What changes is that under a voluntary standard, non-compliant organisations can free-ride while compliant ones carry the cost alone. Making AIGE binding protects organisations already behaving well.
FAQ · part 3

The numbers, the evidence and the model

Q8 · Deepfake fraud is a policing problem, not governance
Policing addresses fraud after money is lost. MAHIR’s Integrity rule addresses it before. The Online Safety Act 2025 already built the reactive layer. MAHIR adds a preventive layer in front: machine-readable provenance on AI-generated media and verified official government channels, so a deepfake can be flagged before it is shared.
Q9 · Is the 685,000 figure an actual count of job losses?
No. It measures workers significantly impacted within three to five years without accelerated reskilling. Exposure, not jobs already lost. MAHIR compares that exposure against places actually funded: 22,000 via RM110 million, only 3.2 per cent coverage. The point is that funding should scale with exposure.
Q10 · Why not simply adopt the EU AI Act?
MAHIR deliberately does not import the EU risk-tier framework wholesale. Every mechanism builds on a Malaysian precedent already in force. A model assembled from instruments Malaysia has already legislated and tested is faster to implement and fits directly into the AI Governance Bill already being drafted.
